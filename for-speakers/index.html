Information for speakers

Give a talk at a PyData Helsinki meetup

Why speak?

Speaking at PyData Helsinki is a great way to share what you know, get feedback on your ideas, and connect with the local data community. Our audience of 80–120 attendees is curious, friendly, and technically diverse – you'll reach people from many different backgrounds and experience levels.

Whether you're an experienced conference speaker or giving your first-ever talk, you are welcome here. We aim to be a supportive space for trying out ideas and practising public speaking.

Talk formats

You can give a regular talk of about 30 minutes, a lightning talk of 5–10 minutes, or propose a debate where the whole audience joins in.

FormatLengthWhat it involves
Regular talkAbout 30 minutesTypically slides and/or live coding on a single topic
Lightning talk5–10 minutesOne idea, tool, or lesson – a great low-effort way to start speaking
DebateOccasionalBring a thought-provoking question or a hot take, and we'll set up a discussion where everyone can take part

We're also open to creative formats – don't let convention stop you.

Thinking about a lightning talk? PyLadies Vancouver has written great tips for giving a lightning talk – like ours, their definition of the format is relaxed, so don't worry about sticking to exactly five minutes. The main points: focus on one idea (what it is, why it matters, and a concrete example), keep slides minimal with large text, practise aloud with a timer, have screenshots as backup if a live demo fails, and end with one takeaway. And remember – you don't need to be an expert to have something worth sharing.

Practicalities

Talks are in English, on topics relevant to data professionals, and not direct product or company marketing. In detail, by default we expect talks to be:

  • About 30 minutes long for a regular talk, or 5–10 minutes for a lightning talk
  • In English
  • About topics relevant to data professionals – Python, R, Julia, and other open-source tools all welcome
  • Not direct product or company marketing
  • In line with the PyData Code of Conduct

What can you talk about?

Good talk topics include tools and libraries you use, lessons from data and ML projects, data engineering and infrastructure, visualisation, what's new in the ecosystem, and developer tools – at any level, from beginner introductions to deep technical dives. In more detail:

  • Tools and libraries. Have you found an interesting tool or library – in Python, R, Julia, or elsewhere – that you use in your daily work? There are so many out there that others likely haven't discovered what you use.
  • Project learnings. If you've worked on a data or ML project – at work or as a hobby – come share what you learned. What worked, what didn't, and what you'd do differently.
  • Data engineering and infrastructure. Pipelines, databases, deployment, MLOps, monitoring – the stuff that keeps data projects running in production.
  • Visualisation and communication. How do you make data understandable? Tools, techniques, and war stories.
  • What's new in the ecosystem. Languages and tools evolve, but it's hard to keep up. Share the new things you've found exciting.
  • Developer tools. Editors, command-line tools, testing frameworks, and other tools useful to data professionals.

Our audience is diverse and there's an appetite for both beginner and advanced material. To get a sense of what our talks look like, check out recordings from past events on our YouTube channel.

Need help?

Yes – we're happy to help with coming up with a topic, structuring your talk, or practising before the meetup. Just reach out and we'll figure it out together.

Typical schedule

A typical meetup evening runs from 17:30 to about 20:30, with three talks, a break with food, drinks, and networking, and a quiz:

17:30Doors open
18:00PyData Helsinki introduction
18:05Welcoming words from the host
18:15Talk 1
18:45Talk 2
19:15Break – food, drinks, networking
19:40The (in)famous PyData Helsinki quiz
19:50Talk 3
20:30End of programme

The exact timings can shift. If you have schedule constraints (arriving from out of town, need to leave early, etc.), let us know and we'll adjust.

Recording

We try to record talks and publish them on YouTube, though we're amateurs at it and can't always guarantee the result. If you'd prefer not to be recorded, that's completely fine – just let us know.

Get in touch

Contact us at pydata-helsinki-organisers@googlegroups.com or on the Helsinki Python Discord if you're interested in giving a talk. We'd love to hear from you.
